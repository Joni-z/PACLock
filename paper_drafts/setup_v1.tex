\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Corpora.}
We evaluate on twelve clinical EEG corpora, chosen so that the evaluation
domain matches the claim: paroxysmal and event-level clinical tasks form the
core, and two sleep-staging corpora are included as a boundary condition on
which cross-frequency content is abundant but not obviously label-relevant.
Seizure detection: TUSZ v2.0.6 \citep{shah2018temple}, CHB-MIT
\citep{shoeb2009application}, and Siena \citep{detti2020eeg}. Event and pattern
classification: TUEV (six-class epileptiform events), IIIC (six-class ictal--
interictal--injury continuum patterns), and TUAR (three-class artifact
labels). Recording-level and cohort-level diagnosis: TUAB (abnormal EEG),
TUEP (epilepsy vs.\ non-epilepsy), ADFD (Alzheimer's disease, frontotemporal
dementia, control), and CAUEEG (normal, mild cognitive impairment,
dementia). Sleep staging: Sleep-EDF (SC subset) and ISRUC. Table~\ref{tab:corpora}
lists task, primary metric, and split protocol; the appendix gives versions,
channel sets, window lengths, and per-split counts. All splits are
subject-disjoint, and where a corpus publishes an official subject-disjoint
split (TUAB, TUEV, TUSZ, TUEP) we use it unchanged.

\paragraph{Preprocessing.}
One frozen protocol is applied to every corpus for our model and for every
from-scratch baseline: 16 bipolar channels of the temporal-central-parietal
montage where the corpus records it, band-pass 0.3--75\,Hz, a 60\,Hz notch
(50\,Hz for European recordings), resampling to 200\,Hz, and division by
100\,$\mu$V. Two corpora keep their established conventions instead:
CHB-MIT is normalized per window and channel by its 95th percentile, and
Sleep-EDF is kept at 100\,Hz with train-set standardization. Every processed
corpus is regenerated from the raw download by the scripts we release, with
a manifest recording version, split membership, and raw-file checksums.
Foundation-model baselines evaluated with their released weights use their
own repositories' preprocessing, since feeding a pretrained model a
different input convention than it was trained on measures the mismatch
rather than the model.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Three groups. (A) Lightweight supervised models --- SPaRCNet, ContraWR,
CNN-Transformer, FFCL, ST-Transformer, EEGNet, and EEGConformer --- trained
from scratch on our protocol, which also calibrate the pipeline against
published numbers on the official TUAB and TUEV splits. (B) EEG foundation
models evaluated with their official pretrained weights and finetuning
recipes: BIOT \citep{yang2023biot}, LaBraM \citep{jiang2024labram}, CBraMod
\citep{wang2025cbramod}, EEGPT \citep{wang2024eegpt}, TFM-Tokenizer
\citep{pradeepkumar2026tfm}, CSBrain \citep{zhou2025csbrain}, and REVE
\citep{bertrand2025reve}. (C) The same foundation-model architectures
trained from scratch on our protocol, which isolates the architecture from
its pretraining. Models whose published numbers we could not reproduce
within two standard deviations of three seeds, or whose validation curve
peaks at the first evaluation (a sign of a mis-specified recipe rather than
a converged model), are reported as such rather than entered into the
comparison. A baseline's recipe is never tuned by us: each model runs the
configuration its authors published for the closest matching task, and on
corpora without a published configuration it inherits the recipe of the
corpus with the same task shape. Where a published protocol differs from
ours --- IIIC in particular, where we deduplicate windows, drop tied votes,
and balance the split --- the difference is disclosed and every model is
scored under the same protocol, so a uniform downward shift of all anchors
is not a bias in either direction.

\paragraph{Our model.}
CroFreMo is trained with AdamW (learning rate $10^{-4}$, weight decay
$10^{-5}$), cosine decay, gradient clipping at 1.0, batch size 32, and the
loss of the corpus (cross-entropy with label smoothing 0.1 for multi-class
tasks, binary cross-entropy or focal loss for the two seizure corpora that
use them), with the number of epochs and evaluation schedule matched to the
from-scratch baselines of the same corpus. Model selection uses the
validation split only; the test split is scored once, at the selected
checkpoint. The pretrained row uses the checkpoint of
Section~\ref{sec:objective} at 60k steps and the standard finetuning recipe
for pretrained EEG models: base learning rate $5\times10^{-4}$ with
layer-wise decay 0.65 by depth, two warmup epochs, and two initial epochs in
which only the tensors not carried by the checkpoint (the patch-50 tokenizer
projections, the head, and the spatial embedding) are trained before the
whole model is unfrozen. This recipe is fixed once for all corpora; it is
not tuned per corpus.

\paragraph{Seeds and reporting.}
Every table cell is the mean and standard deviation over three seeds. The
primary metric per corpus follows the established convention for that
task: AUC-PR for the three seizure corpora, Cohen's $\kappa$ for TUEV, IIIC,
TUAR, and the two sleep corpora, balanced accuracy for TUAB, ADFD, and
CAUEEG, and AUROC for TUEP; balanced accuracy, weighted F1, and AUROC are
reported alongside in the appendix. Learned gate values
($\boldsymbol{\beta}$, $\gamma$ of Eq.~(\ref{eq:duplex})) are reported per
corpus as measurements of how much coupling each task retained.
