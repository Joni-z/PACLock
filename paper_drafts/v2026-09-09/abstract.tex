\begin{abstract}
EEG foundation models tokenize a recording before they model it, and no
current token type represents the relation between two frequency bands.
We put that relation --- phase--amplitude coupling, measured per electrode
and per patch over learned bands --- into the token, with three
guarantees: the interaction token is invariant to the phase reference of
the analysis, preserves band energy exactly, and is gated so that the
uncoupled model is an exactly reachable configuration. We then hold the
tokenizer fixed and vary the encoder. Paired with an encoder that attends
along a frequency axis, or added to CBraMod next to its own spectral
branch, the coupling token is redundant on all but the corpora whose
label is a cross-frequency morphology. Paired with an encoder that has no
cross-frequency pathway at all --- no frequency sub-layer, bands folded
into spatial attention --- the token becomes necessary: removing it costs
$0.136$ AUC-PR on CHB-MIT, $0.107$ $\kappa$ on TUEV, $0.063$ AUC-PR on TUSZ
and $0.027$ $\kappa$ on IIIC. The resulting 2.7M-parameter model, trained
from scratch, is competitive with 25--69M-parameter foundation models on
twelve clinical corpora and ahead of the strongest reproduced baseline by
$0.15$ and $0.09$ AUC-PR on seizure detection; where it loses, it loses
to pretrained or larger models on sleep staging and cohort diagnosis, and
we report those cells rather than average them away. Coupling belongs in
the token when, and because, it is absent from the encoder.
\end{abstract}
