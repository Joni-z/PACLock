\begin{abstract}
EEG foundation models tokenize a recording before they model it, and no
current token type represents the relation between two frequency bands.
We put that relation --- phase--amplitude coupling, measured per electrode
and per patch over learned bands --- into the token. The interaction token
is invariant to the phase reference of the analysis for every band that
has a slower band to align to, preserves each band's amplitude features
exactly, and is gated so that the coupling content can be switched off
within one parameterization. We then hold the tokenizer fixed and vary
the encoder. In a small encoder built around the token --- no frequency
sub-layer, bands folded into spatial attention --- removing the coupling
content costs $0.136$ AUC-PR on CHB-MIT, $0.107$ $\kappa$ on TUEV, $0.063$
AUC-PR on TUSZ and $0.027$ $\kappa$ on IIIC. In place of the native
tokenizer, it lifts LaBraM and REVE trained from scratch by $0.15$ and
$0.24$ $\kappa$ on TUEV; added to CBraMod next to its own spectral branch,
the gains are smaller and corpus-dependent. The 2.7M-parameter model,
trained from scratch, is competitive with 25--69M-parameter foundation
models on twelve clinical corpora and ahead of the strongest reproduced
baseline by $0.15$ and $0.09$ AUC-PR on seizure detection; where it loses,
it loses to pretrained or larger models on sleep staging and cohort
diagnosis. The benefit of explicit cross-frequency content depends on the
encoder's inductive bias, and we report it across four encoders.
\end{abstract}
